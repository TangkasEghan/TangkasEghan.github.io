01 Diagnostic Accuracy of Menstrual Blood for HPV DNA Detection: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis  ·  PROSPERO CRD420260635820; manuscript prepared for the International Journal of Gynecological Cancer PROSPERO registered +

Endah Rahmawati, Ardhanu Kusumanto, Tangkas Eghan, Aqila Adzra, Ghina Athoya, Salsabila Efananda

Division of Fertility and Reproductive Endocrinology, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology; School of Medicine, Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Objective

To evaluate the diagnostic accuracy (sensitivity and specificity) of human papillomavirus (HPV) DNA detection using menstrual blood specimens compared with cervical HPV DNA testing.

Methods

This systematic review followed the PRISMA-DTA guidelines. Eight databases and reference lists were searched through September 2026 for English-language studies evaluating HPV DNA detection in menstrual blood from premenopausal women, using cervical HPV DNA testing or histology as the reference standard. Reviews, case reports and series, animal studies, and studies without sufficient data to construct a 2x2 contingency table were excluded. Risk of bias was assessed with QUADAS-3. Univariate and bivariate random-effects models, subgroup and leave-one-out analyses, and Deeks' funnel plot asymmetry test were performed in R.

Results

Six observational studies involving 3,504 women were included. Pooled sensitivity and specificity were 0.923 (95% CI 0.831-0.967) and 0.848 (95% CI 0.666-0.940), respectively, with significant heterogeneity. Capture sequencing yielded higher sensitivity than PCR-based assays (0.970 vs 0.855; p=0.004), with no residual heterogeneity. Specificity was lower in high-risk clinical populations than in general screening populations (0.709 vs 0.931; p=0.009). The bivariate model yielded similar estimates (AUC 0.945), and leave-one-out analysis confirmed their robustness. Five studies had a high overall risk of bias, mainly due to participant selection.

Conclusion

HPV DNA testing using pad-collected menstrual blood demonstrates high sensitivity and good specificity, particularly with capture sequencing and in general screening populations. As a noninvasive, self-collected specimen, it could help address gaps in cervical cancer screening in Indonesia, although larger studies at low risk of bias are needed.

Keywords: cervical cancer; diagnostic accuracy; HPV DNA; menstrual blood; screening

02 Diagnostic Accuracy of Salivary MicroRNAs for Endometriosis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis  ·  Poster presentation, FRES 2026 Poster presented +

Endah Rahmawati, Tangkas Eghan, Ghina Athoya

Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology; School of Medicine, Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Endometriosis is a chronic, estrogen-dependent inflammatory disease affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age, yet diagnosis is frequently delayed due to non-specific symptoms and the limited accuracy of current non-invasive diagnostic modalities. Salivary microRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as promising non-invasive biomarker candidates; however, evidence on their diagnostic accuracy remains limited and has not been comprehensively synthesized.

Objective

To evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of salivary miRNAs for endometriosis, including pooled sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic odds ratio (DOR), and the area under the hierarchical summary receiver operating characteristic curve (HSROC-AUC).

Methods

A systematic search was conducted across nine electronic databases up to 26 May 2026, following PRISMA-DTA guidance. Eligible studies were assessed for methodological quality using QUADAS-3. Diagnostic data were synthesized using a bivariate random-effects model and an HSROC model. Comparison of miRNA panels versus single-miRNA assays was a post hoc exploratory subgroup analysis.

Results

Three prospective studies (two from France, one from Austria) comprising 434 women (329 cases, 105 controls) met the eligibility criteria. Pooled sensitivity was 0.93 (95% CI 0.79-0.98) and pooled specificity was 0.96 (95% CI 0.87-0.99). The pooled DOR was 201.2, with an HSROC-AUC of 0.917. The 109-miRNA panel achieved higher sensitivity (96.5%) and DOR (737.5) than the single hsa-miR-135a assay (sensitivity 70.6%; DOR 18.0), with significant subgroup differences. All three studies were judged to have an overall high risk of bias.

Conclusion

Salivary miRNA, especially as a multi-miRNA panel, shows promising diagnostic accuracy for endometriosis and may serve as an adjunctive non-invasive screening tool, but it is not yet ready to replace laparoscopy. External, multicenter validation with standardized protocols is needed before clinical implementation.

Keywords: endometriosis; salivary microRNA; diagnostic accuracy; non-invasive biomarker; meta-analysis

03 Efficacy and Safety of Progestin-Primed Ovarian Stimulation versus Gonadotropin Stimulation in Endometriosis Patients Undergoing IVF/ICSI: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis  ·  Presentation, AOFOG 2026 Presented +

Endah Rahmawati, Tangkas Eghan, Ghina Athoya

Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology; School of Medicine, Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Endometriosis affects about 10% of women of reproductive age, and 30-50% of affected women experience infertility. The optimal controlled ovarian stimulation protocol for these women undergoing IVF/ICSI remains controversial. Progestin-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS) has emerged as an oral, potentially lower-cost alternative to GnRH analogue-based protocols.

Objective

To compare the efficacy and safety of PPOS with conventional GnRH agonist and antagonist protocols in women with endometriosis undergoing IVF/ICSI.

Methods

Seven databases were searched up to 20 March 2026. Three reviewers independently screened records and extracted data, and risk of bias was assessed for each study. Dichotomous outcomes were pooled as odds ratios (OR) and continuous outcomes as mean differences (MD) using random-effects models.

Results

Eight studies (seven retrospective, one prospective) involving 3,073 women from France, China, and Turkiye were included. PPOS shortened stimulation duration (MD -0.95 days; 95% CI -1.65 to -0.24), with the largest reduction versus GnRH agonist protocols (MD -2.11 days; 95% CI -2.56 to -1.66). Total gonadotropin dose did not differ overall but was lower than in antagonist-only protocols (MD -319.7 IU; 95% CI -471.0 to -168.4). Oocyte and embryological outcomes were comparable. Clinical pregnancy (OR 0.86; 95% CI 0.64-1.17), implantation (OR 0.90; 95% CI 0.44-1.84), live birth (OR 0.78; 95% CI 0.44-1.41), miscarriage (OR 0.61; 95% CI 0.34-1.11), and ectopic pregnancy (OR 1.03; 95% CI 0.23-4.70) did not differ.

Conclusion

PPOS is an effective and safe alternative to conventional gonadotropin-based stimulation in women with endometriosis undergoing IVF/ICSI. It may shorten stimulation and reduce gonadotropin exposure while maintaining comparable embryological, pregnancy, and safety outcomes. Multicenter randomized trials are needed to confirm long-term clinical and cost-effectiveness benefits.

Keywords: endometriosis; progestin-primed ovarian stimulation; IVF/ICSI; GnRH antagonist; meta-analysis

04 Diagnostic Accuracy of Exercise Stress Electrocardiography for Detecting Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis  ·  Scientific poster, 2026 Poster presented +

A. F. Malika, F. Tsabita, K. T. Kaylie, T. E. Pramudipta, A. D. Kusuma

Medical Doctor Profession Program; Department of Cardiology and Vascular Medicine, Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Ischaemia with non-obstructive coronary arteries (INOCA) is an important cause of angina, yet many patients with non-obstructive findings on angiography remain undiagnosed or are mislabelled as having non-cardiac chest pain. Functional assessment of coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) usually requires specialized equipment, whereas exercise stress electrocardiography is widely available, inexpensive, and routinely used to detect inducible myocardial ischaemia.

Objective

To evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of exercise stress electrocardiography for detecting coronary microvascular dysfunction.

Methods

Seven databases were searched from inception to 15 August 2026. Four reviewers independently screened studies, extracted 2x2 data, and applied QUADAS-2. Sensitivity, specificity, hierarchical summary ROC, and diagnostic odds ratio (DOR) were pooled using random-effects models, with subgroup and leave-one-out analyses.

Results

Of 197 identified records, four studies comprising 818 participants were included. Pooled sensitivity was 59.3% (95% CI 35.9-79.1%) and pooled specificity was 87.6% (95% CI 59.7-97.1%), with an area under the curve of 0.784 and a pooled DOR of 11.87 (95% CI 1.86-75.71). Heterogeneity was substantial across all outcomes, driven mainly by differences in CMD reference standards and phenotype definitions.

Conclusion

Exercise electrocardiography demonstrated moderate diagnostic accuracy for CMD, with greater specificity than sensitivity. Heterogeneity in CMD definitions and testing protocols limits generalizability and supports the need for standardized diagnostic criteria.

Keywords: coronary microvascular dysfunction; INOCA; exercise electrocardiography; diagnostic accuracy; meta-analysis

05 Bridging the Treatment Gap: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis of the Comparative Effectiveness of Telemedicine for Postpartum Depression Systematic Review & Network Meta-Analysis  ·  Scientific paper, AMSA-UGM, 2025 +

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Frengki Prabowo Saputro Wijayanto, Ghina Athoya Thifal, Made Aditya Krisnanta Gandhy, Musa Gani Rahman

Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Introduction

Postpartum depression (PPD) is a serious psychiatric disorder and the most common mental health condition during the puerperium, adversely affecting the well-being of both mother and child. Treatment outcomes remain suboptimal for many patients due to multiple barriers. Telemedicine has emerged as a promising approach to bridge this treatment gap, offering accessible and flexible modalities.

Objectives

To rank telemedicine modalities for the treatment of PPD by their relative effectiveness in reducing depressive symptoms, and to identify factors that may influence treatment outcomes.

Methods

A systematic search was performed in September 2025 across PubMed, Scopus, the Cochrane Library, EBSCOhost, ProQuest, and Epistemonikos for randomized controlled trials evaluating telemedicine modalities in patients with PPD. Unpublished studies and those without accessible full text were excluded. Risk of bias was assessed with the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool, and analysis was performed in RStudio.

Results

Self-guided CBT significantly improved quality of life compared with treatment as usual (TAU) (MD 6.00; 95% CrI 1.24 to 10.81). App-based intervention plus TAU and web-based intervention significantly reduced depression compared with TAU (MD -3.17; 95% CrI -5.93 to -0.45 and MD -2.55; 95% CrI -4.72 to -0.36, respectively).

Conclusions

Telemedicine effectively reduces postpartum depression symptoms, with app-based interventions combined with treatment as usual and self-guided CBT performing best. Integrating culturally adapted, app-based telemedicine into maternal health systems may offer an accessible and sustainable approach to postpartum depression care.

Keywords: effectiveness; postpartum depression; telemedicine; network meta-analysis

06 Accuracy of miRNA as a Predictor of Chemotherapy-Induced Neurotoxicity in Pediatric Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia: A Meta-Analysis Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis  ·  R-Ticles, Vol. 6 No. 2 Published +

Original title: Akurasi miRNA sebagai Prediktor Neurotoksisitas Kemoterapi Leukemia Limfoblastik Akut Anak: Analisis Meta

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Calya Fadey Rashida, Naurelle Tabina Hanief

AMSA-Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) is a common childhood blood cancer with a poor prognosis, especially in Indonesia. Chemotherapy such as methotrexate is standard treatment, but neurotoxicity can reduce patients' quality of life. Early detection matters, yet current screening methods are limited. miRNAs are potentially stable biomarkers that reflect genetic changes related to cancer and nervous system injury.

Methods

This study followed PRISMA-DTA 2020 and included studies on miRNA expression in children with ALL and its association with chemotherapy toxicity. PubMed and Scopus were searched, and analysis was performed in R using univariate random-effects models, with sensitivity analysis to address high heterogeneity.

Results

Based on miRNA single nucleotide polymorphism variants measured in serum or plasma, pooled sensitivity for predicting neurotoxicity was 0.64 (95% CI 0.57-0.70) and pooled specificity was 0.42 (95% CI 0.30-0.54). After sensitivity analysis, sensitivity increased to 0.66 (95% CI 0.59-0.72). The area under the curve was 0.457.

Conclusion

miRNAs have potential as diagnostic biomarkers for detecting neurotoxicity in children undergoing chemotherapy, especially when combined with more specific methods. Their high false-positive rate means they should be used with caution in clinical practice.

Keywords: acute lymphoblastic leukemia; miRNA; neurotoxicity; chemotherapy; meta-analysis

07 The Effect of Traffic-Related Air Pollution and Traffic Noise Pollution on ADHD Symptoms and Cases in Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review Systematic Review  ·  Review article, AMSA-UGM +

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Aqila Adzra Rafifaridha, Jonathan Hamonangan Sihaloho

AMSA-Universitas Gadjah Mada

Objective

To determine the effects of traffic-related air pollution and traffic noise pollution on the number of ADHD cases and the severity of ADHD symptoms in children and adolescents aged 6-18 years.

Methods

This review followed PRISMA. Studies from PubMed and Scopus were identified with predefined keywords and screened against eligibility criteria in Rayyan.ai. Eligible studies were observational cohort and longitudinal studies of children and adolescents aged 6-18 years exposed to traffic-related air and/or noise pollution. Risk of bias was assessed with ROBINS-E. Exposure was measured as traffic-related air pollution, traffic noise in decibels, and distance from residence to the nearest road.

Results

Seven studies met the inclusion criteria and were reviewed using a data extraction table. Several studies carried a moderate risk of bias for different reasons. Some studies showed an association between traffic-related air and noise pollution and ADHD symptoms and cases.

Conclusion

The association between traffic-related pollution and ADHD cannot yet be firmly established, because results across studies were inconsistent and several studies had limitations. The relationship and its mechanisms are therefore not yet fully understood.

08 The Efficacy and Safety of Deferasirox Administration for Children Suffering from Transfusion-Dependent Thalassemia: A Systematic Review Systematic Review  ·  Scientific paper, Indonesian Medical Students' Training and Competition (IMSTC) 2024 +

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta and team

Universitas Gadjah Mada

Introduction

Thalassemia is an inherited disorder caused by mutations in globin genes that decrease globin chain synthesis, leading to abnormal erythrocyte structure and anaemia. Pediatric patients have a high mortality rate. The main conventional treatment is blood transfusion, which carries a risk of iron overload, so iron chelation therapy such as deferasirox is necessary. As a newer drug, its efficacy and safety in pediatric patients have not been fully established.

Objective

To evaluate the ability of deferasirox to reduce iron levels effectively and its possible adverse effects in pediatric thalassemia patients.

Methodology

Eight studies were selected from PubMed using predefined criteria. Rayyan.ai and a data extraction table were used in study selection, and each study was assessed by two reviewers for certainty and risk of bias.

Results and Discussion

Most studies reported decreased serum ferritin levels after deferasirox administration. Several adverse effects related to the kidney, liver, joints, and eyes were reported.

Conclusion

Deferasirox is an effective drug for pediatric thalassemia patients with transfusion-related iron accumulation; however, adverse effects may occur, so the dose must be adjusted for each patient.

10 Successful Pregnancy Following Hysteroscopic Adhesiolysis in a Patient with Asherman's Syndrome: A Case Report Case Report  ·  Manuscript prepared for the Indonesian Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology (INAJOG) Prepared for INAJOG +

Endah Rahmawati, Shofwal Widad, Tangkas Eghan

Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology; School of Medicine, Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Introduction

Asherman's syndrome (AS) is characterized by intrauterine adhesions that typically develop after postpartum or postabortal curettage and may lead to menstrual irregularities, infertility, and recurrent pregnancy loss. This report presents a successful full-term pregnancy following hysteroscopic adhesiolysis in a patient with severe AS caused by multiple sharp curettages, and discusses key considerations in prevention and management.

Case Illustration

A 29-year-old G4P0A3 woman with three early pregnancy losses and five prior postabortal sharp curettage procedures presented with hypomenorrhea and recurrent pregnancy loss. Ultrasonography, hysterosalpingography, and hysteroscopy confirmed severe intrauterine adhesions (Manchanda's Endoscopic Centre grades 2 and 1). Hysteroscopic sharp adhesiolysis was performed, followed by intrauterine poloxamer-chitosan gel and estrogen-progestin therapy. Re-adhesions developed and were managed with a second adhesiolysis, poloxamer-chitosan instillation, and a copper-T intrauterine device. Six months later the patient conceived spontaneously and delivered a healthy term infant without major complications.

Conclusions

Severe AS due to multiple sharp curettages can be effectively treated with hysteroscopic adhesiolysis, anti-adhesion measures, and structured postoperative management and surveillance. Early recognition of menstrual abnormalities and subfertility, prompt hysteroscopic evaluation, and favoring medical management or manual vacuum aspiration over sharp curettage are crucial for optimizing reproductive outcomes.

Keywords: Asherman syndrome; hysteroscopy; intrauterine adhesion; adhesiolysis

11 The Association of Problematic Pornography Use with the Severity of Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Symptoms among Preclinical Medical Students in Indonesia Undergraduate Thesis (Cross-Sectional Study)  ·  Undergraduate thesis, defended November 2026 Defended +

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta

Bachelor of Medicine Program, Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Depression, anxiety, and stress symptoms are common among medical students, particularly in the preclinical phase. Problematic Pornography Use (PPU), characterized by loss of control and continued use despite negative consequences, has been suggested to be associated with these symptoms. Evidence on this association among medical students in Indonesia remains very limited.

Objective

To examine the association between PPU and the severity of depression, anxiety, and stress symptoms among preclinical medical students in Indonesia.

Methods

Secondary analysis of a 2025 cross-sectional study by the R-Project Research Team of AMSA-Indonesia among preclinical medical students from 43 universities (n=356). PPU was measured with the Problematic Pornography Consumption Scale (PPCS-18; score >=76), and symptoms with the Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Scale (DASS-21), categorized as normal-mild or moderate-extremely severe. Associations were analyzed using Poisson regression with robust variance to obtain prevalence ratios (PR) and adjusted PRs (aPR), adjusting for sex, age, semester, and monthly allowance.

Results

Among 356 participants (mean age 19.24 years; 53.9% female), PPU prevalence was 7.6% (95% CI 5.3-10.8). Moderate-to-extremely severe symptoms were found in 46.9% for anxiety, 41.6% for depression, and 18.0% for stress. After adjustment, PPU was significantly associated with depressive symptoms (aPR 2.09; 95% CI 1.64-2.66) and anxiety symptoms (aPR 1.90; 95% CI 1.54-2.35), but not with stress (aPR 1.60; 95% CI 0.83-3.09). A higher monthly allowance was associated with all three outcomes. Sex, age, and semester were not significantly associated with any outcome.

Conclusion

PPU was associated with the severity of depression and anxiety symptoms among preclinical medical students in Indonesia after adjustment for sex, age, semester, and economic status, but not with the severity of stress.

Keywords: problematic pornography use; depression; anxiety; stress; medical students

12 Neuroprotective Potential of Cinnamomum verum Extract against Post-Stroke Cognitive Impairment in an Ischemic Stroke Model: A Molecular, Cellular, and Memory Function Analysis Original Research (In Vivo Study)  ·  PKM-RE, PIMNAS 37 (2024); manuscript prepared for journal submission PIMNAS 37 +

Iffah Zakiya Yasmin, Erisa, Khansa Audrey Haliza Rahmatania, Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Ghina Athoya Thifal, Nur Arfian

Faculty of Pharmacy; Faculty of Medicine, Public Health and Nursing, Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Ischemic stroke therapy with tissue plasminogen activator carries a risk of cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury, which damages the hippocampus and causes post-stroke cognitive impairment in 60% of stroke patients. The neuroprotectant citicoline did not significantly protect the brain. Cinnamomum verum has known neuroprotective activity.

Objective

To analyze the effect of C. verum extract on memory function, and on cellular and molecular markers of antioxidant enzymes, apoptosis, and inflammation in the hippocampus of an ischemic stroke model.

Methods

C. verum was extracted in 70% ethanol. Rats underwent transient bilateral common carotid artery occlusion for 20 minutes followed by reperfusion. Extract doses of 100, 200, or 400 mg/kg were administered every 2 days for 4 doses. Memory function was tested with the Morris water maze and hippocampal morphology with hematoxylin-eosin staining. mRNA expression of SOD1, SOD2, caspase-3, and NF-kB was analyzed by RT-PCR, and Bax and CD68 proteins by immunohistochemistry.

Results

Compared with the occlusion-only group, the C. verum groups showed shorter distance and escape latency, higher SOD1 expression, and lower NF-kB, Bax, and CD68 expression.

Conclusion

C. verum extract has neuroprotective potential through improved spatial memory function and antioxidant, antiapoptotic, and anti-inflammatory activity in ischemic stroke models.

Keywords: Cinnamomum verum; post-stroke cognitive impairment; antioxidant enzyme; apoptosis; inflammation

Background

Liver fibrosis develops in almost all chronic liver diseases and, if untreated, progresses to irreversible cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. No antifibrotic therapy has yet been approved by the FDA. Purple sweet potato contains phenolic compounds, anthocyanins, and flavonoids with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity, and fermentation further increases its total phenolic content and antioxidant activity.

Objective

To explore the hepatoprotective potential of fermented purple sweet potato juice in preventing the progression of liver fibrosis in a bile duct ligation mouse model, through molecular, cellular, and liver function analyses.

Mice are allocated to five groups (n=5 each): sham operation, bile duct ligation without treatment, and bile duct ligation with fermented juice at 35, 70, or 140 mg/kg. Expected Outcome

Evidence on fermented purple sweet potato juice as a locally sourced functional food that can attenuate oxidative stress, apoptosis, inflammation, and fibrosis progression in the liver.

15 V-ENTRIX: A Smart Sequentially Released Nano-in-Micro Composite of Indacaterol, Tiotropium, and Nicotine Delivered in an E-Cigarette as a Holistic Therapy for Smokers with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Innovation Essay  ·  Popular science essay, 18th Scientific Atmosphere 2026 (1st Place) 1st Place +

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Ghina Athoya Thifal

Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is among the three leading causes of death worldwide, and smoking is its main risk factor. Indonesia has about 70 million active smokers. Guidelines recommend inhaled bronchodilators and nicotine replacement therapy for smokers with COPD, but effectiveness is limited by poor inhaler technique, which affects up to 80% of patients, and by nicotine dependence.

Proposed Innovation

V-ENTRIX is a therapeutic device in a vape-pod form factor that delivers a nano-in-micro composite powder with sequential release. Indacaterol is loaded in solid lipid nanoparticles within fast-dissolving PVP microparticles for rapid onset; tiotropium is loaded in PLGA nanoparticles within mannitol-PVP microparticles for 24-hour release; and nicotine is loaded in chitosan-TPP nanoparticles to blunt peak plasma levels and support gradual tapering. Characterization would use dynamic light scattering, zeta potential, scanning electron microscopy, encapsulation efficiency, in vitro release in simulated lung fluid, and cascade impactor testing.

Conclusion

By combining three agents with distinct release profiles, V-ENTRIX targets lung function and nicotine dependence at the same time. Challenges remain in formulation complexity, dosing precision, and public perception, but the concept merits consideration as a future therapy for smokers with COPD.

Keywords: COPD; smoking cessation; nano-in-micro composite; indacaterol; tiotropium

Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Marcellino Maatita

Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Post-herpetic neuralgia is chronic neuropathic pain that persists for months to years after a herpes zoster rash heals, and its incidence rises with age. Neuroinflammation driven by pro-inflammatory (M1) microglia after peripheral nerve damage is thought to underlie the chronic pain.

Proposed Innovation

STRENGTHEN is a sprayable thermosensitive hydrogel containing minocycline encapsulated in biodegradable PLGA nanoparticles. Minocycline protects nerve tissue by inhibiting the activation and migration of M1 microglia, inhibiting CX3CR1, and reducing inflammatory cytokines. Nanoparticle encapsulation improves skin permeation and supports retrograde transport of the drug toward the affected nerves, while the spray format allows practical local application.

Conclusion

STRENGTHEN is a potential preventive approach for post-herpetic neuralgia, particularly in elderly patients. Its effective topical dose, delivery device, and efficacy still need to be established through formulation studies, in vitro and in vivo testing, and clinical trials.

17 RESOLUTE: Glucose-Responsive Nanocarrier Insulin Delivery in a Hollow Microneedle Patch Integrated with pH, Glucose, and Ketone Sensors as a Therapy for Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus Innovation Essay  ·  Scientific essay, MEDJONSON 2025 +

Original title: Inovasi RESOLUTE: Glucose-responsive Nanocarrier Insulin Delivery in Hollow Microneedle Patch Integrated with pH, Glucose, and Ketone Sensor sebagai Terapi Pasien Diabetes Melitus Tipe 1

Muhammad Ikram Alfarizi, Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta

Universitas Gadjah Mada

Background

Type 1 diabetes mellitus is an autoimmune disease causing absolute insulin deficiency. Its burden is increased by diabetic ketoacidosis, a life-threatening emergency, while insulin correction under current standard therapy can cause hypoglycemia when monitoring is imprecise.

Proposed Innovation

RESOLUTE is a hollow microneedle patch delivering insulin through a glucose-responsive nanocarrier. When blood glucose rises, glucose oxidase converts glucose to gluconic acid, which triggers insulin release automatically. The patch also integrates colorimetric sensors for pH, glucose, and ketone bodies, giving patients an early warning of diabetic ketoacidosis. The microneedles would be characterized by fracture force testing, flow rate measurement, and skin irritation testing.

Conclusion

By combining simple microneedle application, automated glucose-responsive insulin release, and real-time pH, glucose, and ketone monitoring, RESOLUTE is a potential approach to improve the effectiveness of insulin therapy in type 1 diabetes, pending preclinical and clinical testing.

Keywords: type 1 diabetes; hollow microneedle; glucose-responsive insulin; ketone sensor; diabetic ketoacidosis

18 CATCHPV: A Minimally Invasive HPV Treatment Innovation Abstract (Synthetic Biology)  ·  BIOS 2025 +

Erin Larasati Angdika, Mariel Chrysantha Tampubolon, Jordan Ariel Indarta, Nicholas Sidik, Daniel Stenly Chandra, Rayhan Devishkar Al Mahmud, Claire Xing, Tangkas Eghan Pramudipta, Ruben Wijaya, Ketut Rayana Rahadiva Diana

International student collaboration, including Universitas Gadjah Mada

Abstract

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is an etiological factor for cervical cancer, which remains the leading cause of cancer-related deaths among Indonesian women, with over 36,000 new cases and 21,000 deaths each year. HPV initiates infection through binding of its L1 capsid protein to heparan sulfate proteoglycans on epithelial cells. While many infections are benign, persistent high-risk types such as HPV-16 and HPV-18 cause about 70% of cases worldwide. Despite vaccination and screening, treatments such as surgical excision remain invasive, costly, and limited in accessibility.

Proposed Innovation

CATCHPV is a minimally invasive treatment system designed to reduce cervical HPV load and the chance of progression to cancer. A genetically engineered M13 bacteriophage expresses anti-L1 antibody variable domains on its capsid proteins and is anchored to the surface of a cervical cap to capture HPV particles, which are then physically removed with the cap. A second engineered M13 bacteriophage targets infected cervical cells, where the presence of HPV activates the phage to eliminate the infected cell before it can become cancerous.

Conclusion

By capturing virus particles and eliminating infected cells, CATCHPV offers a patient-friendly alternative to conventional HPV treatments. Its simple self-insertion procedure reduces discomfort and reliance on specialized clinical procedures, making it a potentially accessible complement to vaccination and screening programs, particularly in low-resource settings.

Keywords: HPV; cervical cancer; cervical cap; bacteriophage-based therapy; minimally invasive treatment
